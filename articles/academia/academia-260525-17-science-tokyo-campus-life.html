Campus Life Around Tokyo Tech and Science Tokyo: Student Life in a Residential Area of Tokyo

Published May 25, 2026

Tokyo Tech is now part of Institute of Science Tokyo, commonly branded as Science Tokyo. For students searching for “Tokyo Tech student life,” the key point is still practical: the science-and-engineering campus experience is centered around places such as Ookayama, Yokohama, and Tamachi, each with a different rhythm.

Quick summary

  • Tokyo Institute of Technology merged with Tokyo Medical and Dental University to form Science Tokyo on October 1, 2024.
  • Ookayama is a residential Tokyo campus area rather than a Shibuya-style entertainment district.
  • The former Suzukakedai Campus was renamed Yokohama Campus effective April 1, 2026.
  • Students should check whether their laboratory is at Ookayama, Yokohama, Tamachi, or another Science Tokyo campus.
  • Science Tokyo provides current-student and international-student guidance online.

This is an independent campus-life guide

This article is written for international students, graduate applicants, researchers, and professionals who want to understand daily life around Science Tokyo / Tokyo Tech. It is not an official university page, and it does not rank universities or campuses. The goal is to explain what student life may feel like around the campus area and what practical points to check before moving to Japan.

Campus locations, dormitory availability, student support systems, transportation routes, and local prices can change. As of May 2026, applicants should always confirm the latest information on official university pages and through the relevant graduate school, international office, or laboratory.

Tokyo Tech, Science Tokyo, and what students should call it

Many applicants still search for “Tokyo Tech” because the name is internationally familiar. Officially, Tokyo Institute of Technology merged with Tokyo Medical and Dental University to form Institute of Science Tokyo on October 1, 2024. The brand name Science Tokyo is now used by the new institution.

For students, this naming change matters when reading websites, application pages, and campus guides. Some information may still appear on former Tokyo Tech pages, while newer information may be on Science Tokyo sites.

When contacting a laboratory or office, use the official current name when appropriate, but do not be surprised if professors, alumni, and older pages still refer to Tokyo Tech.

Ookayama: technical campus life in residential Tokyo

Ookayama is located in Meguro, Tokyo, and has long been the symbolic center of the former Tokyo Tech experience. It is not a large entertainment district. It is a relatively residential area with station access, small shops, restaurants, and daily conveniences.

This is an advantage for many science and engineering students. You can live in Tokyo without feeling that every day is crowded tourism or nightlife. Campus, station, supermarket, apartment, and laboratory routines can be compact and efficient.

The surrounding area also gives access to wider Tokyo by train. Students can reach major districts when they want to, but normal life can remain calmer and more study-focused.

Yokohama Campus: research-focused and more suburban

Science Tokyo has changed the former Suzukakedai Campus name to Yokohama Campus, effective April 1, 2026. This campus is located in Yokohama and is important for research centers, laboratories, and science-and-engineering work.

The lifestyle around Yokohama Campus differs from Ookayama. It can feel more suburban and research-focused, with more attention to commuting and laboratory schedule. Students who imagine “Tokyo life” should check whether their actual lab is here.

For some graduate students, Yokohama Campus may be excellent because housing can be more manageable and the campus environment can support focused research. For others, the distance from central Tokyo may matter socially.

Tamachi and central access

Tamachi Campus is located in Minato, Tokyo, close to central business areas and railway access. It does not create the same everyday campus-town feeling as Ookayama, but it can be convenient for professional education, innovation activities, and city connections.

Students should not assume all Science Tokyo campuses feel similar. Tamachi is central and urban; Ookayama is residential and academic; Yokohama is more research-campus oriented. The best housing location depends on which of these appears in your weekly schedule.

This is especially important for graduate students who may need to visit different campuses for seminars, administrative procedures, or collaborations.

Daily life for technical students

Student life around Science Tokyo is often shaped by technical training. Many students spend long hours in laboratories, design rooms, computer environments, or seminars. The campus-life question is not only “what is nearby?” but “can I maintain a sustainable routine?”

Ookayama’s residential setting can help. Students can keep a regular schedule, shop nearby, commute efficiently, and still access Tokyo’s broader cultural and professional opportunities. International students should also use official student-life and international-student guides for procedures, safety, housing, and campus support.

Compared with universities in smaller cities, costs can be higher. Compared with central Tokyo campuses, however, residential neighborhoods may provide a more balanced environment.

Who will enjoy Science Tokyo / Tokyo Tech life?

This environment suits students who want serious science and engineering training while living in Tokyo without being immersed in the busiest urban areas every day.

The main advice is simple: confirm the exact campus. “Tokyo Tech” or “Science Tokyo” can mean Ookayama, Yokohama, Tamachi, or another campus, and each produces a different student life.
